AGEING NAVIGATOR


MVP PRODUCT & DEVELOPER BUILD BRIEF
FOUNDING PILOT — SIMPLIFIED VERSION 1
September 2026




MVP GOAL
Build the simplest possible working version of Ageing Navigator that allows an older person or family member to:


1. Choose what they need help with.
2. Answer a short set of relevant questions.
3. Receive a useful personalised Action Plan based on verified New Zealand information.
4. Give Ageing Navigator enough information to review the plan and learn from the pilot.
5. Request human Implementation Support if they want help putting the plan into action.


The first version is for approximately 10–20 pilot families.


The purpose is to prove that the navigation and Action Plan are useful before investing in a larger platform.




1. PRODUCT CONCEPT


Ageing Navigator is an independent New Zealand ageing-navigation service.


It helps older people and their families understand:
• what their situation means;
• what living and care options may be worth considering;
• what government support or funding pathways may be relevant;
• what they should do next;
• which organisations or services may be able to help; and
• when formal professional assessment or advice is required.


The first version should feel intelligent and personalised without being technically complex.




The MVP is NOT:
• a chatbot;
• a comprehensive ageing assessment;
• a provider marketplace;
• a CRM;
• a family portal;
• a persistent Family Ageing Record;
• a live vacancy platform;
• an autonomous AI adviser; or
• a finished national platform.


It is essentially:


PATHWAY SELECTION
→ CONDITIONAL QUESTIONNAIRE
→ RULES + VERIFIED KNOWLEDGE
→ PERSONALISED ACTION PLAN
→ FEEDBACK
→ OPTIONAL IMPLEMENTATION SUPPORT




2. PURPOSE OF THE FOUNDING PILOT


The purpose of Version 1 is learning.


We need to establish:


• whether families understand the proposition;
• whether they complete the questionnaire;
• whether we are asking the right questions;
• whether the resulting Action Plan is genuinely useful;
• whether the advice and pathways are accurate;
• what information is missing from our repository;
• what families most commonly need help with;
• whether families request Implementation Support;
• what implementation tasks actually involve;
• how much human time different implementation tasks take; and
• whether organisations are willing to refer families to Ageing Navigator.


Revenue optimisation is not the primary purpose of the first 10–20 cases.


Implementation may initially be offered free or on a pilot basis in return for feedback.




3. MVP PRODUCT PRINCIPLE


KEEP IT SIMPLE.


Every feature must help us answer one of two questions:


1. Can Ageing Navigator produce a useful personalised Action Plan?
2. What do families want human help implementing afterwards?


If a feature does not help us answer those questions, it probably belongs in Version 2.




4. USER JOURNEY


STEP 1 — ARRIVAL


The person arrives at the existing Ageing Navigator landing page.


They may come from:
• Home Carers;
• a retirement village;
• residential care provider;
• social worker;
• hospital;
• GP;
• community organisation;
• Google/search;
• social media;
• direct referral.


Where practical, a simple referral-source parameter or code should be captured.
A sophisticated referral-partner management system is NOT required.
STEP 2 — CHOOSE WHAT THEY NEED HELP WITH


The user sees clear pathway/module choices.


Example:


“What would you like help with?”


Possible Version 1 pathways:


• Living & Care Options
• Government Funding & Needs Assessment
• Staying Safely at Home
• Coming Home From Hospital
• Memory & Dementia
• I’m Not Sure Where to Start


These are not separate software products.


They are different pathways through the same questionnaire system.




STEP 3 — CONDITIONAL QUESTIONNAIRE


The system asks only questions relevant to the selected pathway and previous answers.


Questions may use:
• single choice;
• multiple choice;
• Yes / No / Unsure;
• ranges;
• dates;
• short written answers.


The form must support conditional branching.


Example:


Question:
“Has a formal needs assessment been completed?”


If YES:
Ask when and whether needs have changed.


If NO:
Do not ask assessment-result questions.


If UNSURE:
Record that this needs to be checked and reflect this in the Action Plan.




CORE PRINCIPLE:


Every question must earn the right to be asked.


If an answer does not materially affect:


• the recommended pathway;
• funding guidance;
• priority;
• Action Plan;
• safety consideration; or
• implementation recommendation,


do not ask it.


Financial questions should only be shown when relevant.




STEP 4 — GENERATE PERSONALISED ACTION PLAN


After completing the questionnaire, the user receives a personalised Action Plan.


The Action Plan should be structured rather than an uncontrolled AI-generated essay.


See Section 7 for the required format.




STEP 5 — FEEDBACK


Immediately after receiving the plan, ask a very short feedback survey.




STEP 6 — IMPLEMENTATION SUPPORT


The Action Plan displays:


“Would you like help putting your plan into action?”


The family can request human Navigator support.


Implementation is handled manually outside the application during the pilot.


No case-management platform or payment system is required for Version 1.




5. NAVIGATION PATHWAYS / MODULES


The MVP should support different pathways but the underlying technical implementation should remain simple.


Recommended pathways:


A. LIVING & CARE OPTIONS
Purpose:
Help someone understand which living/support pathways may be worth exploring.


Potential outcomes could include:
• remain at home with additional support;
• explore retirement village living;
• explore retirement village options with future care;
• investigate care-suite options;
• seek a formal needs assessment;
• explore residential aged care;
• identify areas requiring further information.


The system must not independently determine formal residential-care eligibility or clinical care level where professional assessment is required.




B. GOVERNMENT FUNDING & NEEDS ASSESSMENT
Purpose:
Help families understand:
• whether a needs assessment may be required;
• how to approach the local assessment organisation;
• what information to prepare;
• potentially relevant funded support;
• Residential Care Subsidy pathways;
• Residential Care Loan pathways;
• what remains unknown;
• who makes the formal decision.




C. STAYING SAFELY AT HOME
Purpose:
Explore:
• current support;
• home help;
• personal care;
• mobility;
• falls;
• equipment;
• meals;
• transport;
• companionship;
• family support;
• respite;
• home safety;
• whether further assessment may be useful.








D. COMING HOME FROM HOSPITAL
Purpose:
Help families think through:
• discharge;
• support at home;
• mobility;
• equipment;
• meals;
• medication support;
• transport;
• family availability;
• short-term private support;
• formal assessment;
• immediate safety concerns.




E. MEMORY & DEMENTIA
Purpose:
Help organise the situation and next steps without diagnosing.
This may include:
• observed changes;
• safety;
• support available;
• GP involvement;
• dementia organisations;
• respite;
• home support;
• future living considerations;
• EPOA considerations.




F. I’M NOT SURE WHERE TO START


A short broader questionnaire identifies the main areas that appear to require attention.


The resulting Action Plan can cover more than one pathway.


The user should NOT have to complete multiple separate questionnaires during the first session.




6. SIMPLE QUESTION & RULE SYSTEM


The developer does not need to build a sophisticated SaaS module framework.


A simple structured question/rule system is sufficient.


Each question should contain fields such as:


• Question ID
• Pathway
• Question text
• Answer type
• Answer options
• Required / optional
• Show when / conditional rule
• Action or rule triggered


Example:


Question ID:
FUND_004


Pathway:
Funding & Needs Assessment


Question:
Has a formal needs assessment been completed?


Answer:
Yes / No / Unsure


Show when:
Funding pathway selected OR residential-care pathway triggered


Rule:
If No or Unsure, consider assessment preparation as an Action Plan priority.




QUESTIONS SHOULD BE EDITABLE WITHOUT MAJOR REDEVELOPMENT WHERE PRACTICAL.


However, a large custom admin/configuration platform is not required.


A simple database, spreadsheet-style admin system or lightweight backend is acceptable.




7. PERSONALISED ACTION PLAN


This is the main MVP output.


The plan should include:




A. YOUR CURRENT SITUATION


A short plain-English summary based only on information supplied by the user.




B. WHAT MATTERS MOST


Summarise the person's stated priorities, concerns and immediate goal.




C. PATHWAYS WORTH EXPLORING


For example:


• staying at home with additional support;
• retirement village living;
• care-suite options;
• needs assessment;
• residential care;
• further professional advice.


Where more than one option is reasonable, show more than one.


Do not present uncertain conclusions as facts.




D. YOUR PRIORITY ACTIONS


Each action should explain:


• What to do
• Why it matters
• What the next step is
• Who may help
• What to prepare
• Questions to ask




E. FUNDING / SUPPORT THAT MAY BE RELEVANT


Where applicable, explain potentially relevant funding/support pathways.


The plan must clearly distinguish between:


• information Ageing Navigator can provide; and
• formal eligibility decisions made by government or assessment organisations.




F. ORGANISATIONS / SERVICES THAT MAY HELP


Where appropriate, include relevant:


• government organisations;
• local services;
• community organisations;
• home-care providers;
• retirement villages;
• residential-care providers;
• dementia organisations;
• professional advisers.




G. THINGS WE STILL NEED TO CHECK


If the user has selected Unsure or information is missing, clearly show this.


Example:


“We still need to confirm whether a formal needs assessment has already been completed.”




H. PROFESSIONAL ASSESSMENT / ADVICE


Clearly state where:


• clinical assessment;
• legal advice;
• financial advice;
• formal needs assessment; or
• government eligibility determination


is required.




I. IMPLEMENTATION SUPPORT


End with:


“Would you like help putting your plan into action?”


Button:


REQUEST NAVIGATOR SUPPORT




ACTION PLAN OUTPUT


The Action Plan should:


• display clearly on screen;
• be mobile friendly;
• be available as a downloadable PDF;
• use consistent Ageing Navigator branding.




8. USE OF AI


AI may be used to:


• summarise questionnaire information;
• convert structured recommendations into natural family-friendly wording;
• produce the current-situation summary;
• explain approved information clearly;
• assist with formatting the Action Plan.


AI must NOT independently invent:


• government eligibility rules;
• funding thresholds;
• clinical conclusions;
• legal advice;
• financial advice;
• provider information;
• care levels;
• government processes.


Underlying advice must come from:


QUESTION ANSWERS
+
CONFIGURED RULES
+
VERIFIED KNOWLEDGE REPOSITORY


AI is primarily the communication layer, not the source of truth.




9. KNOWLEDGE REPOSITORY — VERSION 1


The Action Plan must use a small verified repository.


For the pilot, this will initially focus on Tauranga / Bay of Plenty plus relevant national information.


Initial topics should include:


• Support Net / needs assessment;
• funded home and community support;
• Residential Care Subsidy;
• Residential Care Loan;
• residential-care funding/cost rules;
• EPOA process;
• Tauranga retirement villages;
• Tauranga care suites;
• Tauranga residential aged-care providers;
• Tauranga private home-care providers;
• dementia support;
• respite;
• mobility/equipment;
• transport;
• relevant community organisations.




SIMPLE KNOWLEDGE RECORD


Each entry should contain:


• Topic
• Region / jurisdiction
• When it applies
• Approved family explanation
• Process / next step
• Organisation/contact information
• Official or verified source
• Last checked
• Status: Current / Needs Review


Manual management of the repository is acceptable for Version 1.


Automated source monitoring is NOT required.




10. FAMILY / PILOT RECORD


Version 1 does NOT require:


• family accounts;
• login;
• persistent Family Ageing Record;
• return-user history;
• multi-user family access;
• automatic updating of old information.


For each completed questionnaire, Ageing Navigator simply needs an internal record containing:


• name/contact information where supplied;
• questionnaire answers;
• selected pathway;
• referral source if available;
• generated Action Plan;
• feedback;
• whether Implementation Support was requested.


This can be stored securely in a simple database/admin area.


The purpose is for the Ageing Navigator team to review the first 10–20 cases and improve the service.




11. AGEING NAVIGATOR ADMIN VIEW


A lightweight admin area is required.


For each pilot case we need to see:


• family/contact details;
• selected pathway;
• questionnaire answers;
• generated Action Plan;
• date completed;
• referral source;
• family feedback;
• implementation request.


Admin should also be able to:


• update common questionnaire content;
• update repository information;
• view/export pilot data.


A sophisticated dashboard is NOT required.


CSV export is sufficient for analysis.




12. FAMILY FEEDBACK


After receiving the Action Plan, ask:


1. Did this help you understand what to do next?


2. Did it identify anything you had not thought about?


3. Was anything important missing?


4. What would you most like help with now?


5. Optional comments.


Feedback must be visible to Ageing Navigator and exportable.




13. IMPLEMENTATION SUPPORT


The Action Plan should include:


“Would you like help putting your plan into action?”


The user can select:


YES — I WOULD LIKE NAVIGATOR SUPPORT


Ask:


“What would you most like help with?”


Allow multiple selection / open text.


Examples may include:


• arranging a needs assessment;
• preparing for an assessment;
• funding applications;
• organising EPOA;
• arranging home support;
• finding residential care;
• checking care-home availability;
• helping with a care transition;
• contacting organisations;
• coordinating next steps;
• other.




PILOT IMPLEMENTATION


Implementation will be handled manually by Ageing Navigator.


The application does NOT need:


• payment processing;
• Navigator allocation;
• task management;
• communication tools;
• case-management CRM.


Ageing Navigator will separately record:


• what help was requested;
• what tasks were completed;
• organisations contacted;
• Navigator time spent;
• barriers encountered;
• outcome;
• whether further help was needed.


This data will help determine future paid Implementation Services and pricing.




14. REFERRAL SOURCE


We want to learn which organisations actually refer families.


Version 1 only needs simple referral-source tracking.


Options:


• URL parameter;
• simple referral code;
• dropdown asking “How did you hear about us?”


Example:


ageingnavigator.com/?ref=VillageA


The case should then record:


Referral source = Village A


A full partner-management dashboard is NOT required.




15. PRIVACY & SECURITY


Although Version 1 is simple, privacy remains important.


Requirements:


• HTTPS;
• reputable managed hosting/database;
• secure admin access;
• collect only information required for navigation;
• clear privacy/consent wording;
• do not ask for exact financial information unless genuinely required;
• ranges should be used where sufficient;
• no family information visible to other users;
• personal information not used to train external AI systems;
• appropriate safeguards around AI API use;
• secure handling of questionnaire answers and Action Plans.


Because there are no persistent user accounts, Version 1 should avoid collecting unnecessary information.




16. SUGGESTED TECHNICAL APPROACH


The exact technology stack is open to developer recommendation.


Preference is for:


• common technologies;
• fast development;
• low ongoing cost;
• easy maintenance;

Possible architecture:


FRONT END
Mobile-responsive questionnaire and Action Plan interface.


QUESTION DATA
Simple structured database / configuration.


RULE LOGIC
Conditional questions and Action Plan triggers.


KNOWLEDGE REPOSITORY
Structured approved information.


AI API
Controlled generation of family-friendly wording.


ACTION PLAN
Web output + PDF generation.


ADMIN
Simple secure view of cases, responses, feedback and repository.


There is no requirement to build enterprise-level architecture for the pilot.

20. MVP ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA


Version 1 is complete when:


• A family can open the service on mobile.


• They can select the area they need help with.


• Only relevant questions are shown.


• “Unsure / Need to check” answers are supported.


• The questionnaire produces a coherent personalised Action Plan.


• Advice is based on configured rules and verified repository information.


• AI does not invent eligibility, clinical or financial conclusions.


• Relevant organisations/resources can be shown.


• The Action Plan clearly identifies priorities and next steps.


• The family can download the Action Plan as a PDF.


• Ageing Navigator can view the questionnaire answers and generated plan.


• The family can provide feedback.


• The family can request Implementation Support.


• Ageing Navigator can identify how the family was referred where possible.


• Pilot information can be exported for analysis.

25. DEVELOPER SUMMARY


We are NOT looking for a developer to build a large aged-care SaaS platform at this stage.


We need a simple working product that does one thing very well:


A family chooses what they need help with, answers a short intelligent questionnaire and receives a genuinely useful personalised Action Plan based on verified New Zealand information.


Ageing Navigator must then be able to review the questionnaire and Action Plan, collect feedback and identify whether the family wants help implementing the recommendations.


Different navigation pathways are required, but they can operate through one simple conditional questionnaire and rules system.


The first 10–20 families will tell us what should be built next.


The priority for Version 1 is:


SIMPLE TO BUILD
+
SIMPLE FOR FAMILIES
+
ACCURATE ACTION PLANS
+
USEFUL PILOT DATA
+
CLEAR IMPLEMENTATION DEMAND


Anything beyond that should be treated as Version 2.

Ageing Navigator Brand Guide
Brand identity and everyday usage • Version 1 • 2 October 2026




Purpose
Use this guide to keep Ageing Navigator consistent across email, the website, Action Plans,
presentations and partner materials. The identity combines clear guidance with a calm,
approachable appearance.

Brand role
Ageing Navigator helps older people and their families understand care and future living
options, identify practical next steps and access coordination support. The brand must
work across home support, retirement villages and residential care.

Identity meaning
The curved path represents different journeys and practical next steps. The directional point
represents guidance. Deep teal is the main colour; gold provides a restrained accent. The
name stays prominent so the logo remains readable at everyday sizes.

Name and independence
Always write Ageing Navigator, using British English spelling. Explain the ownership
relationship with Home Carers transparently where relevant. Use consistent matching
criteria; do not imply provider endorsement or preferential treatment.

Colour balance
Use white or ivory for most page space, teal for identity and emphasis, and slate for longer
text. Keep gold occasional. The HEX values are the digital colour specification; generated
raster artwork may contain slight tonal variation.

Readable combinations
Teal on white is approximately 9.9:1; slate on white is approximately 12.2:1. Gold on white is
approximately 2.8:1 and should not be used for small text. For normal website text, aim for at
least 4.5:1 contrast. Avoid placing teal lettering on a dark background.

Typography
Use Poppins SemiBold for headings, Poppins Medium for short labels where available, and
Poppins Regular for body text. Standard documents use 11 pt body text with single line
spacing. Website body text should generally begin at 18 px and be tested with users.

Use Arial for live email-signature text when Poppins is unavailable. The wordmark is artwork:
do not retype or substitute its lettering.

Usage and communication
Spacing and minimum size
Leave clear space around the complete logo equal to at least half the height of the capital
A in Ageing. Keep other text, edges and graphics outside this area. For the full logo, use a
practical minimum display width of 220 px; 260–300 px is preferred in email. Retain its
aspect ratio.

Email signature
Recommended file: Ageing_Navigator_white_medium.png. Insert it at 300 px wide,
preserving proportions. The transparent medium file is suitable on a confirmed light
background. A white-backed version provides more predictable separation in email clients,
although dark-mode behaviour still needs testing.

Keep your name, title, phone number and website as live text. Use Arial at 11–12 pt, slate for
text and teal for links. Suggested order: Paul John McLaren; Founder | Ageing Navigator;
phone; ageingnavigator.com. Add only confirmed contact details and clickable links. Test
desktop, mobile and dark mode.

Brand voice
Write in British English. Be calm, practical, clear and respectful. Speak to older people as
decision makers. Explain options and the next manageable action. Use personalised,
organisation and ageing. Avoid fear-based messaging, guaranteed outcomes and
unsupported authority claims.

Suitable wording: “Understand your options and identify practical next steps.” Keep the free
Action Plan distinct from coordination and future paid tools. Explain professional
assessment or advice requirements where relevant.

Keep the identity consistent
Do not stretch, rotate, recolour or add effects to the logo. Avoid busy photo backgrounds
and low contrast. Do not attach a small tagline to the email logo. Use the supplied artwork
rather than recreating it from a screenshot.

Files and future production
The logo pack contains three colourways in three sizes, all PNG. PNG is suitable for email
and digital use; transparent PNG preserves an alpha channel. These are raster assets, not
editable vectors. For signage, embroidery or large-format print, commission a faithful SVG
master and agree printer-specific CMYK values from this digital palette.

Keep this guide and the logo pack together. Update the version date when colours,
typography or approved artwork change.

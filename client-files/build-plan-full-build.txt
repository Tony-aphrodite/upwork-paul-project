1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

Ageing Navigator is an independent New Zealand ageing-navigation and transition service.

The core premise is:

Something has changed. What should happen next?

The trigger may include:

- dementia diagnosis;
- stroke;
- hospital admission or discharge;
- fall;
- increasing frailty;
- mobility problems;
- difficulty managing the home;
- loss or illness of a spouse/carer;
- increasing support needs;
- considering retirement living;
- considering residential care;
- or simply planning ahead.

The user often does not know what support they need or whether their current living
situation remains appropriate.

Ageing Navigator helps them consider three primary pathways:

1. Stay at Home
2. Retirement Village
3. Residential Care
The core navigation service remains free.

The user receives a personalised Action Plan and, where appropriate, matching with
relevant services, retirement villages or residential-care facilities.

Ageing Navigator has three commercial layers:

FREE NAVIGATION
↓
PAID SPECIALIST DECISION TOOLS
↓
PAID TRANSITION IMPLEMENTATION

The user can:

- implement the free Action Plan themselves;
- purchase a specialist $99 digital tool where deeper financial analysis is useful;
- or ask Ageing Navigator to coordinate the transition for them as a paid service.

2. PURPOSE OF THE MVP

The MVP should test both the navigation model and the commercial model.

We need to learn:

- whether users complete the questionnaire;
- whether pathway recommendations make sense;
- whether the Action Plan is useful;
- whether provider/village matching is useful;
- whether users will pay for deeper specialist analysis;
- whether $99 is an acceptable price;
- which paid tool generates the strongest demand;
- whether paid-tool customers subsequently request implementation;
- what information is missing;
- which implementation services users request;
- how much human time implementation requires;
- whether paid transition services can be delivered profitably;
- and which distribution sources generate paying customers.

The MVP must remain simple, flexible and inexpensive to modify.

10. "I'M NOT SURE" PATHWAY

- Stay at Home;
- Retirement Village;
- Residential Care

before starting.

The system may recommend more than one option.

For example:

11. CONDITIONAL QUESTIONNAIRE
The questionnaire should be dynamic.

Users should only see questions relevant to their circumstances.

Examples:

If dementia is selected:
→ ask relevant cognition, safety, support and future-planning questions.

If the user has a formal residential-care assessment:
→ ask assessed care level.

If no assessment has occurred:
→ do not ask questions that assume an assessment exists.

If retirement living is relevant:
→ ask detailed matching questions.

Users should be able to answer:
- Unsure
- Don't know
- Not applicable

where appropriate.

The system must not require users to guess information they do not know.

13. DECISION RULES

Recommendations should be rules-led.

AI should not independently determine:

- required clinical care level;
- formal government eligibility;
- legal conclusions;
- financial advice;
- clinical conclusions.

Examples:

IF user needs household help but is managing personal care
→ consider Stay at Home support.

IF future care is important
→ prioritise villages with suitable future-care options.

IF formal residential-care assessment exists
→ use the assessed care level as a matching requirement.
IF professional assessment is required
→ clearly identify this in the Action Plan.

Rules should be editable without substantial redevelopment.

15. SOURCE HIERARCHY

Where information changes over time, Ageing Navigator should use a source hierarchy.

2. Health NZ / relevant health authorities
3. Official regional services
4. Official provider documents
5. Verified provider websites
6. Other reliable secondary sources where required

Every important rule or financial figure should remain traceable to its source.

Where information conflicts, it should be flagged for manual review rather than
automatically resolved by AI.

20. FREE MATCHING ENGINE

Matching should initially be rules based.

The objective is not to create a mathematically perfect ranking.

The objective is to produce a useful shortlist and explain why each option may fit.

For example:

VILLAGE A

Why it matched:

- preferred area;
- preferred accommodation type;
- pet policy appears suitable;
- future-care options available.

Something to consider:

- limited transport options.

Commercial relationships must not determine matching or ranking.

Ageing Navigator should not accept payment from providers to rank higher.

28. FREE VS PAID BOUNDARY

The distinction must be clear.

FREE AGEING NAVIGATOR

Should provide enough value to be useful on its own.

This may include:

- likely pathway;
- broad funding pathways;
- service/village matches;
- broad cost considerations;
- priority next steps.

$99 SPECIALIST TOOLS

Provide deeper analysis requiring:

- additional questions;
- structured financial information;
- detailed calculations;
- personalised scenario comparison;
- deeper funding analysis;
- preparation information.

The paid product must not simply hide information that should reasonably be part of the
free Action Plan.

58. FINAL PRODUCT SUMMARY

Ageing Navigator should not initially become a large aged-care software platform.

The MVP has four jobs:

1. Help families understand what is happening.
2. Help them identify the most relevant pathway.

3. Give them useful independent information and matching.

4. Test whether they will pay for deeper analysis or for Ageing Navigator to coordinate the
transition.

The business model is:

FREE NAVIGATION
+
$99 SPECIALIST DECISION TOOLS
+
PAID TRANSITION IMPLEMENTATION

The key distinction is:

FREE:

Help me understand my options and find suitable choices.

$99:

Help me understand the financial implications or funding pathways in much greater detail.

PAID IMPLEMENTATION:

Help me make it happen.

The free and paid layers should share the same:

- questionnaire architecture;
- rules engine;
- verified repository;
- provider repository;
- AI controls;
- source-document structure;
- report-generation system.

They should not be built as separate standalone applications.

The first priority should be the Retirement Village pathway because it allows Ageing
Navigator to test:

- free matching;
- financial repository data;
- the first $99 specialist tool;
- paid digital conversion;
- and Retirement Village Transition enquiries

from the same underlying pathway.

The Tauranga pilot should then determine what is expanded, changed, automated or
removed before Ageing Navigator scales nationally.

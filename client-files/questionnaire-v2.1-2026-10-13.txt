[NEW] Version 2.1 website-entry changes — 13 October 2026
* [UPDATED] The website “What has brought you here?” cards are now the official Entry Pathway and determine which branch appears first.
* [REMOVED / REPLACED] The questionnaire no longer repeats the old “What has prompted you to look for help or information now?” question.
* [UPDATED] Respite, EPOA, Retirement Village and Residential Care direct-entry logic has been clarified so users do not repeat decisions they already made on the website.

Key changes from Version 1
[UPDATED] The Navigator is now modular. A family may trigger several relevant modules at once rather than being forced into a single Stay at Home / Retirement Village / Residential Care pathway.
[NEW] Respite screening has been added to the main Navigator, with a route to a dedicated Respite Care module when more detail is needed.
[NEW] EPOA status is checked for every family because many families do not realise it should be addressed until a crisis. The main Navigator identifies the gap; the detailed EPOA Preparation Pack sits outside the free plan.
[UPDATED] Basic free matching may still show suitable providers/villages/care homes and explain why they fit. Detailed pricing, financial terms, availability and comparison belong in the paid specialist tools.
[UPDATED] The paid-tool routes are now: Home Support Provider Comparison, Retirement Village Comparison, Care Home Comparison, Funding Eligibility & Preparation, and EPOA Preparation Pack.
[UPDATED] “Implementation” has been replaced with “Coordination”. The core paid coordination services are Home Support Search & Coordination, Retirement Village Search & Coordination, Residential Care Search & Coordination, plus smaller Respite and EPOA coordination packages.
[REMOVED / REPLACED] The old model of treating Stay at Home, Retirement Village and Residential Care as the only three possible pathways has been replaced by a main direction plus additional modules/actions.

1. Purpose
The questionnaire should help Ageing Navigator understand:
what has changed;
the older person's current circumstances;
what support may be needed;
what matters most to them;
[UPDATED] which living/support options are worth exploring;
[NEW] which additional modules are relevant, including Funding, Respite and EPOA;
[UPDATED] which providers or retirement villages may appear to match their stated preferences at a basic level;
what practical actions should appear in their Action Plan.
[UPDATED] The questionnaire should not diagnose, clinically assess, determine formal care levels, determine funding eligibility, provide legal advice, or tell somebody definitively where they should live.
The main question Ageing Navigator should help answer is:
What should we explore or do next?
Target completion time: approximately 6–10 minutes for most users. Questions should be conditional so people only see what is relevant.
2. Who Can Complete It
The questionnaire should work equally well for:
the older person;
spouse or partner;
son/daughter;
another family member;
friend;
volunteer;
advocate;
professional/support person.
Where possible, wording should adapt between “you” and “the person you’re helping”.
[NEW] 3. Modular Navigation Model
[NEW] The Main Navigator remains the front door, but the output can activate more than one relevant module. A family does not need to fit one rigid pathway.

[NEW] A user may also enter directly through a focused Respite or EPOA module. Direct-entry logic is set out in the Appendix.
[NEW] Website Entry Pathways — first routing layer
[NEW] The website screen “What has brought you here?” is now the official start of the Navigator. The selected card must be stored as Entry Pathway and used to decide which questions appear first after the common Q1–Q4 questions.
[NEW] Website Entry Pathways:
* A dementia diagnosis → start with Memory & Cognition.
* A hospital admission or discharge → start with Current Living Situation and Formal Support & Assessment.
* A fall, stroke or change in mobility → start with Mobility & Safety.
* Managing at home is getting harder → start with Staying at Home / Home Support.
* A partner or family carer is struggling → start with Current Support / Respite.
* Support or care needs are increasing → start with Current Support / Assessment / Home Support.
* You need respite or a break from caring → direct to the focused Respite Care module.
* You're considering a retirement village → start with Retirement Living.
* You're exploring residential care → start with Residential Care.
* You want to organise an EPOA → direct to the focused EPOA Navigation module.
* You want to plan ahead → use the broader planning route.
* I'm simply not sure → use the broader Main Navigator route.
[UPDATED] The Entry Pathway determines where the conversation starts, not the final recommendation. Answers can still activate several modules or a different main direction.
[UPDATED] Do not repeat the website pathway question inside the questionnaire. Q5 is now used only to identify other issues that may also matter.

[UPDATED] 4. Free Matching vs Paid Specialist Tools
[UPDATED] The free Action Plan may include a basic shortlist of providers, villages or care homes where reliable data exists. Matching should use transparent non-financial criteria such as location, support needs, lifestyle, interests, pets, accommodation preferences, mobility/accessibility and future-care preferences.
[UPDATED] Each free option should explain why it matched. The free Navigator should not attempt the full detailed comparison that belongs in the paid specialist tool.
[NEW] Paid specialist tools:
Home Support Provider Comparison: Compare services, availability, pricing, minimum hours, location and fit.
Retirement Village Comparison: Compare fit, entry cost, ongoing fees, DMF/exit costs, future-care options and longer-term cost.
Care Home Comparison: Compare assessed care-level fit, dementia support, availability, accommodation/premium charges and key differences.
Funding Eligibility & Preparation: Identify potential public funding/support pathways, documents, decision-maker, process and next steps.
EPOA Preparation Pack: Prepare key information, proposed attorneys, decisions/questions, official-form information where appropriate, and a lawyer-ready summary.

[UPDATED] FREE ACTION PLAN OUTPUT
1. Your Current Situation — What has changed and where things currently stand.
2. What Matters Most — The family’s main priorities.
3. Your Main Options — Potentially Stay at Home, Retirement Village, Residential Care, or more than one option.
4. Important Things to Address — Relevant modules such as EPOA, Respite, Funding, Needs Assessment or carer support.
5. Your Priority Actions — For each relevant action: What to do; Why it matters; Next step; Who can help; What to prepare; Questions to ask.
6. Basic Provider / Village / Care Home Matches — Where relevant and where reliable data exists. Explain why each option appears to match.
7. Things to Check — Information that still needs confirmation.
8. Professional Advice / Assessment — Clear direction where clinical, legal, financial or formal needs-assessment input is required.
9. Relevant Ageing Navigator Tools — Direct the family to the relevant detailed paid tool or focused free module.
10. Coordination Option — Ask whether Ageing Navigator should help search, organise, contact, arrange and follow through on agreed next steps.

[NEW] APPENDIX A — FOCUSED MODULE ENTRY LOGIC
[UPDATED] The website Entry Pathway can send a family into any relevant first branch. Respite and EPOA remain focused direct-entry modules that can be completed without the entire Main Navigator. All routes should reuse common questions where possible.
[NEW] A0. Website Entry Pathway — first routing step
[NEW] The website screen “What has brought you here?” is the first routing step. Save the selected card as the Entry Pathway before Q1 begins.
[NEW] Routing after Q1–Q4:
* A dementia diagnosis → Memory & Cognition first.
* A hospital admission or discharge → Current Living Situation + Formal Support & Assessment first.
* A fall, stroke or change in mobility → Mobility & Safety first.
* Managing at home is getting harder → Staying at Home + Home Support first.
* A partner or family carer is struggling → Current Support + Respite first.
* Support or care needs are increasing → Current Support + Formal Support & Assessment + Staying at Home/Home Support first.
* You need respite or a break from caring → Direct Respite Care module (A1).
* You're considering a retirement village → Retirement Living first.
* You're exploring residential care → Residential Care first.
* You want to organise an EPOA → Direct EPOA Navigation module (A2).
* You want to plan ahead → Broader planning questions, then relevant modules.
* I'm simply not sure → Broader Main Navigator questions; allow multiple modules to emerge.
[UPDATED] After the first branch is explored, continue with common checks and any additional modules triggered by the answers. The Entry Pathway does not determine the final Action Plan by itself.
[UPDATED] Direct Respite and EPOA entry should not force the family through the full Main Navigator unless their answers indicate broader navigation would be helpful.

[NEW] A1. Respite Care Module — minimum questions
[NEW] Who are you completing this for? (reuse Q1–Q4 where needed)
[NEW] What type of respite are you looking for?
[NEW] Where is respite needed?
[NEW] When is it needed and for how long?
[NEW] What support/care does the person need during respite?
[NEW] Are there dementia, mobility, personal-care, medication or overnight-support considerations?
[NEW] Are you looking for at-home respite, day respite, facility respite, or are you unsure?
[NEW] Contact details and consent.
[NEW] Output: suitable respite options where reliable information exists; clearly separate “appears suitable” from “currently available”. Availability should be date-stamped and provider-confirmed/recently verified wherever possible. Offer Respite coordination if the family wants Ageing Navigator to contact providers and progress the arrangement.
[NEW] A2. EPOA Navigation Module — minimum questions
[NEW] Are Property and Personal Care & Welfare EPOAs already in place?
[NEW] If yes, do you know where the signed documents are held?
[NEW] If not in place, is the person able to participate in making their own decisions? (Do not assess capacity; if uncertain, direct to legal advice.)
[NEW] Do they already have a lawyer/solicitor they use?
[NEW] Do they understand the two EPOA types and what still needs to be decided?
[NEW] Would they like the EPOA Preparation Pack?
[NEW] Would they like Ageing Navigator to coordinate finding a legal professional, arranging the appointment, gathering documents and following up?
[NEW] Free output: explain the purpose of EPOA, identify what still needs to be checked/decided, and direct the family to appropriate legal help. Paid EPOA Preparation Pack: gather key information/proposed attorneys/considerations, prepare a lawyer-ready summary and official-form information where appropriate. Ageing Navigator does not give legal advice, determine who should be appointed, certify capacity or witness execution.
[UPDATED] TARGET LENGTH
[UPDATED] The website Entry Pathway should reduce repetition by taking the user straight to the most relevant branch. Most users should still complete the Main Navigator within approximately 6–10 minutes through branching.
[UPDATED] Simple situation: approximately 15–20 questions
[UPDATED] One main direction plus one additional module: approximately 20–25 questions
[UPDATED] Complex situation with matching and several modules: approximately 25–30 questions
If pilot data shows completion taking materially longer, remove or defer questions before adding more.
